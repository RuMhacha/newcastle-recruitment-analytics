# Sandro Tonali Succession Analysis

## Question

The objective was to identify credible succession options for Sandro Tonali while distinguishing between players who were statistically similar and those who remained attractive once evidence strength, development potential and recruitment risk were considered.

This created a two-stage recruitment problem:

1. Which players most closely matched the required succession profile?
2. Which of those candidates represented the strongest recruitment opportunities after uncertainty and risk were incorporated?

## Initial Analysis

The initial succession model identified several plausible statistical replacements for Sandro Tonali, but it did not produce a single dominant candidate.

Mario Dorgelès recorded the highest initial succession score at 64.4. Victor Froholdt followed at 61.4, with Irakli Yegoian at 61.3, Jordan Holsgrove at 61.1, Max Ejdum at 61.0 and Kodai Sano at 60.9. The relatively narrow spread among much of the candidate group suggested that statistical similarity alone provided limited separation between the leading options.

This initial ranking was useful for identifying players whose on-pitch profiles warranted further investigation, but it treated the statistical result as stronger evidence than the underlying data necessarily justified. Similar succession scores could be supported by different levels of evidence, development potential and recruitment risk.

The initial output was therefore treated as a candidate-generation stage rather than the final shortlist. A second decision layer was used to test whether the apparent statistical fit remained convincing once evidence strength, development potential and recruitment risk were incorporated.

## Decision Analysis

The decision analysis materially reshaped the Tonali succession shortlist. Rather than relying on statistical similarity alone, candidates were reassessed using evidence confidence, development potential and recruitment risk to produce a final risk-adjusted score.

Victor Froholdt emerged as the strongest overall candidate. His score increased from 61.4 to 76.0, moving him decisively ahead of the initial field. Kodai Sano showed a similarly strong improvement, rising from 60.9 to 74.5, while Irakli Yegoian increased from 61.3 to 72.6. These movements elevated all three players into the priority-target tier.

Max Ejdum also strengthened his position, moving from 61.0 to 67.2, while Angelo Stiller and Jordan Holsgrove both reached 65.5. Michał Skóraś improved more moderately from 60.5 to 63.0, leaving these candidates as credible alternatives rather than the leading recruitment priorities.

The most significant reversal concerned Mario Dorgelès. Despite recording the highest initial succession score at 64.4, his risk-adjusted score fell to 58.3. Patrick Wimmer declined from 60.3 to 52.0, while Quinten Timber fell from 60.8 to 50.3. Their initial statistical similarity therefore did not translate into equally strong recruitment cases once the wider decision criteria were considered.

The resulting shortlist demonstrates why the initial succession score was treated as a screening mechanism rather than a final recruitment ranking. The decision layer created substantially greater separation between candidates and shifted the recommendation towards players whose statistical fit was supported by stronger overall recruitment cases.

## Key Finding

The strongest initial statistical match was not the strongest overall recruitment option.

Mario Dorgelès led the initial succession ranking with a score of 64.4, but fell to 58.3 once the wider decision criteria were incorporated. In contrast, Victor Froholdt, Kodai Sano and Irakli Yegoian all began with lower initial scores but strengthened substantially after adjustment, finishing at 76.0, 74.5 and 72.6 respectively.

The result demonstrates that relatively small differences in statistical similarity can conceal much larger differences in recruitment suitability. For Tonali's succession, the decision layer did more than reorder a shortlist: it separated a tightly grouped set of statistical matches into clearly differentiated recruitment priorities.

This reinforces the role of the succession model as a candidate-generation tool rather than a final decision mechanism. Statistical fit identifies who warrants investigation; the risk-adjusted layer helps determine where recruitment resources should be focused.

## Recommendation

Newcastle should prioritise Victor Froholdt for deeper recruitment investigation, with Kodai Sano and Irakli Yegoian forming the remaining priority-target group.

Froholdt produced the highest risk-adjusted score at 76.0 and the largest positive movement among the leading candidates, providing the strongest overall case for progression to detailed scouting and recruitment due diligence. Sano and Yegoian also strengthened materially and should be investigated alongside him rather than treated as secondary statistical alternatives.

Max Ejdum, Angelo Stiller and Jordan Holsgrove should remain on the wider shortlist as credible alternatives, while Michał Skóraś warrants further validation before being elevated into the leading group.

Mario Dorgelès should not be prioritised solely because he produced the highest initial succession score. His downgrade after risk adjustment illustrates why the statistical ranking should inform candidate discovery without determining the final recruitment recommendation.

## Limitations

This analysis is designed to support candidate prioritisation rather than produce a definitive transfer recommendation. Statistical similarity and risk-adjusted scoring cannot fully capture tactical adaptability, positional flexibility, physical characteristics, personality or how effectively a player would transition into Newcastle's playing environment.

The results are also dependent on the quality and comparability of the available performance data. Differences between leagues, tactical systems, player roles and sample sizes may influence both the initial succession scores and the strength of the evidence supporting individual candidates.

Recruitment feasibility is represented only at a high level. Transfer fees, wages, contract situations, availability, injury history and a player's willingness to move would require separate investigation before any recruitment decision.

The final shortlist should therefore be treated as a structured basis for further scouting and due diligence. Quantitative analysis can help determine where recruitment resources should be focused, but final decisions should combine the model output with video analysis, live scouting, financial assessment and contextual football expertise.
