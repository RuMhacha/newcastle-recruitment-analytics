# Bruno Guimaraes Succession Analysis

## Question

If Newcastle United needed to replace Bruno Guimarães, which players from the wider multi-league recruitment pool should be prioritised as potential successors?

The objective was not simply to identify the player most statistically similar to Guimarães. The analysis was designed to distinguish between players who appeared suitable from their on-pitch profiles and those whose cases remained convincing once evidence strength, development potential and recruitment risk were considered.

This created a two-stage recruitment problem:

1. Which players most closely matched the required succession profile?
2. Which of those candidates represented the strongest recruitment opportunities after uncertainty and risk were incorporated?

## Initial Analysis

The initial succession model identified several plausible statistical replacements for Bruno Guimarães, but it did not produce a single dominant candidate.

Darío Osorio recorded the highest initial succession score at 67.2. Nilson Angulo followed at 64.9, with Rihito Yamamoto at 63.9. Thomas Jørgensen, Pablo Gozalbez, Richard Ríos and Walid Ould-Chikh formed a relatively compressed group behind the leading candidates.

The initial ranking provided a useful starting point for candidate generation, but the differences between several players were relatively small. A higher succession score therefore indicated stronger statistical alignment with the target profile, rather than sufficient evidence that the player should automatically become the preferred recruitment option.

This distinction was important because statistical similarity alone does not account for differences in evidence confidence, development potential or recruitment risk. The initial shortlist was therefore treated as a screening stage rather than a final recommendation.

A second decision layer was subsequently applied to test whether the apparent statistical fit remained convincing once those wider recruitment considerations were incorporated.

## Decision Analysis

![Bruno Guimarães succession candidates — initial suitability versus final risk-adjusted decision score](../figures/bruno_decision_comparison.png)

The decision layer materially changed the shape of the succession shortlist. Thomas Jørgensen and Rihito Yamamoto emerged as the two strongest priority targets despite neither player recording the highest initial succession score.

Jørgensen increased from an initial score of 62.7 to a risk-adjusted score of 74.8, the largest positive movement among the leading candidates. Yamamoto similarly improved from 63.9 to 72.3. Their movement indicates that the broader recruitment evidence strengthened their cases beyond what player similarity alone suggested.

Darío Osorio remained a strong option, moving from 67.2 to 68.1, but his initial statistical advantage was no longer sufficient to make him the leading recommendation once the wider decision criteria were considered.

The model also separated several superficially comparable candidates. Stije Resink and Abubakr Barry improved into the scout-and-validate tier, while Richard Ríos declined to the watchlist. Patrick Wimmer, Walid Ould-Chikh and Pablo Gozalbez experienced substantially larger downgrades, indicating that their initial statistical suitability was not supported strongly enough by the wider recruitment assessment.

## Key Finding

The principal finding is that the highest statistical similarity score did not identify the strongest recruitment target.

Darío Osorio led the initial assessment, but the risk-adjusted decision model instead prioritised Thomas Jørgensen and Rihito Yamamoto. This reversal demonstrates why the initial succession score is better interpreted as a candidate-generation mechanism than as a final ranking.

The strongest recruitment decisions emerged only after statistical suitability was considered alongside evidence confidence, development potential and recruitment risk.

## Recommendation

Thomas Jørgensen should be treated as the primary recruitment target, with Rihito Yamamoto retained as a second priority option.

Both candidates warrant progression to deeper scouting and due-diligence work because their final risk-adjusted scores materially strengthened their initial statistical cases. Darío Osorio should remain on the strong shortlist and provides a credible alternative, but the current evidence does not justify placing him ahead of Jørgensen or Yamamoto.

Stije Resink and Abubakr Barry should remain in the scouting pipeline pending further validation. The remaining watchlist candidates should not currently consume the same level of recruitment resource unless new evidence materially changes their profiles.

## Limitations

The decision scores should be interpreted as recruitment-support indicators rather than forecasts of future performance.

The model depends on the quality, coverage and comparability of the underlying player data. Differences between leagues, tactical roles and sample sizes may affect both similarity and succession scores, while development potential and recruitment risk necessarily introduce additional modelling assumptions.

The analysis also does not fully capture transfer fee, wage expectations, contract availability, injury information, personality, tactical adaptability or live scouting evidence. These factors would need to be incorporated before a final recruitment decision.

Accordingly, the shortlist is intended to prioritise where Newcastle should direct further scouting and due diligence rather than to automate the transfer decision itself.
